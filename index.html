PhD Student in Geology

Indiana University Bloomington

I am a PhD student in Geology at Indiana University Bloomington, working in sedimentology and stratigraphy. My primary research interest is understanding deposition, erosion, and flocculation of fine-grained sediments like siliciclastic and carbonate clays, silts, and mud in the lab through flume experiments. Thereby, the knowledge can be applied to the field observations of black shales and carbonate deposits worldwide.

About Me

I am a PhD student in Geology at Indiana University Bloomington, advised by Prof. Jürgen Schieber. My research lies at the intersection of sedimentology, geochemistry and petroleum geology.

I am particularly interested in using mathematical and computational approaches to understand how complex biological patterns and structures emerge from interactions at the cellular level. My work combines mathematical modeling, numerical simulation, parameter inference, and quantitative analysis of biological data.

Research

Zebrafish Pattern Formation

I use agent-based models and parameter inference methods to study the cellular interactions underlying pigment pattern formation in zebrafish.

Fern Growth and Cell Dynamics

I develop computational models of cell growth and division to investigate how local cellular dynamics contribute to tissue-level growth and morphology in fern gametophytes.

Topological Data Analysis of Angiogenesis

I apply topological data analysis and computational methods to quantify structural features of vascular networks and investigate differences in angiogenesis.
